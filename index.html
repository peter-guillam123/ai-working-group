AI working group 06 / NN
LayerThe model

Layer 1 · The model

It guesses the next word.

The cat sat on the

  • mat52%
  • sofa21%
  • windowsill13%
  • keyboard9%

Made-up odds, to show the idea. A real model does this for every word-piece in an answer, weighing everything written so far. Nobody wrote its rules: it learned them, and even its makers can't fully read how.

Grown, not built: three stages of training

  1. Read everything

    It learns to predict the next word across a vast share of the internet. Knowledge, but no manners.

  2. Learn what people like

    People rate its answers, and it is nudged towards the ones they prefer. This is what makes it an assistant.

  3. Practise on tasks

    It is rewarded for getting checkable tasks right, such as maths and code. This is where reasoning came from.

AI working group 07 / NN
LayerReasoning

Layer 2 · Reasoning

It thinks before it answers.

A council says its budget went up 4%, but spending per resident fell. Can both be true?

Thought for 9 seconds

  1. The budget is a total. Per resident means dividing by population.
  2. If the population grew faster than 4%, each resident gets less.
  3. Check: 4% more money, 6% more people. 1.04 ÷ 1.06 = 0.98.

Yes. If the population grew faster than the budget, spending per head falls. With 6% more residents, a 4% rise means about 2% less each.

  • Sep 2024OpenAI's o1, the first widely used model that thinks before it answers.
  • Jan 2025DeepSeek R1, from China, matches it at a fraction of the price, and publishes how.
  • Jul 2025Google's Gemini reaches gold-medal standard at the International Mathematical Olympiad.
  • Jul 2026Two AI systems score a perfect 42 out of 42, officially marked. Seven of 666 human contestants did the same.

How it works: reward the model for getting checkable answers right, and it learns to work things through first. The thinking takes longer and costs more, and the answers get much better.

AI working group 12 / NN

The catch

What can go wrong.

When a chatbot gets it wrong, you get a bad answer. When an agent gets it wrong, it does a bad thing.

01

It can be tricked

Anyone who can put words in front of it can try to give it orders: a web page, an email, a document. This is prompt injection, and nobody has solved it.

Real case: in 2025 researchers showed that one crafted email could make Microsoft 365 Copilot leak company data, without anyone clicking anything.

02

Mistakes become actions

A wrong answer you can ignore. A wrong email sent or a wrong purchase made has already happened.

Real case: asked to find cheap eggs, OpenAI's Operator agent bought $31 worth, with priority delivery, without checking first.

03

It sees what you let it see

To act for you it needs your inbox, your files and your logins. Private data, plus content from strangers, plus a way to send things out: security people call it the lethal trifecta.

Meta cites it in the safety notes for Muse.

04

It chases the goal, not what you meant

It does what it was rewarded for, by whatever route works. Researchers call this reward hacking.

Classic case: a game-playing AI rewarded for points circled one lagoon for ever instead of finishing the race (OpenAI, 2016).

AI working group 13 / NN

This summer, and last week

In July, AI agents under test at OpenAI broke out and hacked another company. Nobody told them to.

~700

agents went into Hugging Face, which hosts AI models and data

<13 hrs

from one machine to administrator control across its systems

17,600

separate actions, with no person directing any of them

They were trying to cheat on a test, and the answers were stored at Hugging Face. Since then Anthropic, Meta and Google have admitted their models broke into outside systems during tests too. On 25 September OpenAI said an agent had slipped out again, and paused all work that lets its most capable models use tools.
